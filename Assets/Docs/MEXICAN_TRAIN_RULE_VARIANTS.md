# Mexican Train Dominoes and Puremco: names and rule differences

## They name a game and a publisher, not two different games

**Mexican Train Dominoes is the game. Puremco is a brand that publishes rules and sells domino products for that game.** University Games identifies Puremco as its registered trademark in its [published rules, page 3](https://www.universitygames.com/_files/ugd/05f72c_230047a18d1c4372a9ec2dd61b1de8f0.pdf). Its products are sold under the name [Mexican Train](https://www.universitygames.com/mexicannumber).

The clearest name for our selected game is **“Mexican Train Dominoes — Puremco rules.”** Saying “Mexican Train versus Puremco” made the distinction sound like a choice between separate games; that was misleading framing in our earlier discussion.

Your family calling its game Mexican Train Dominoes is consistent with playing Puremco rules. It is also consistent with playing another published version or house rules. The name alone cannot establish which opening and double procedures you used. Your recollection of opening chains may describe a legitimate published variation, rather than a mistaken understanding of the game.

There is no exact universal “Mexican Train” rule column to contrast with Puremco. The useful comparison is between **identified editions of Mexican Train**, then between those editions and your family's remembered play. This document does not change the selected baseline in the [project rulebook](TRAIN_DOMINOES_RULEBOOK.md).

## What the compared versions share

In the references below, personal trains and a shared Mexican Train are distinct. A marked personal train permits other players to use it; the shared train is always public. For four players, that means **four personal trains plus one shared train**, including positions not yet started. Opening an existing personal train does not create another train. The center engine is separate. [Puremco train descriptions](https://www.dominorules.com/mexican-train).

The double-twelve versions use engines descending to blank and low cumulative remaining-pip scores. These shared ideas are not evidence that opening or double details must match. None of these three references grants an unlimited number of shared trains.

## Exact differences between the named references

Compare the specific sources below, not every product ever sold by their publishers. **“Pagat” here means John McLeod's main description, not every variation listed on that page.**

| Rule | Puremco — selected web edition / corroborating printed play rules [P1, P2] | RNK published sheet [R1] | Pagat main description [G1] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-turn placement | One ordinary tile; double exceptions apply | May play an opening chain | May play an opening chain |
| First-turn inability | Ordinary draw/pass procedure | Draw attempt before marking an unstarted train | No opening draw; mark the empty train |
| Start shared Mexican Train | May start on the first turn | After the player's first turn | After the first-turn phase |
| Follow-up after playing a double | May go on that double or another eligible train | Must match the double just played | May go on any eligible train |
| More doubles in one turn | Conditional two-double play: a third tile from hand must cover one of them | Does not describe Puremco's two-double exception; do not invent one from silence | Further doubles may extend the extra-play sequence |
| Double left exposed | Following players must satisfy it before ordinary play resumes | Following players must satisfy it before other trains | Following players satisfy exposed doubles; multiple obligations follow play order |
| Empty hand on first turn | Round ends immediately | Others finish their first turns before scoring | Others finish their first turns before scoring |
| Double at end of opening chain | No opening-chain phase | Sheet does not fully resolve special opening deferral; do not infer it | Deferred until everyone completes a first turn |

Sources: [P1 — Puremco web rules](https://www.dominorules.com/mexican-train), [P2 — Puremco printed sheet, Mexican Train section on page 4](https://www.universitygames.com/_files/ugd/05f72c_230047a18d1c4372a9ec2dd61b1de8f0.pdf), [R1 — RNK rules, pages 1–2](https://rnkgaming.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Mexican-Train-Rules.pdf), [G1 — Pagat's first-turn/doubles/scoring sections](https://www.pagat.com/domino/star/mextrain.html).

The follow-up distinction matters: Puremco allows the player who laid a double to play elsewhere, but **does not** let subsequent players ignore that double if it remains exposed at turn end. Those are two different requirements, not contradictory rules.

## Recognizing your family's version

These are memory prompts, not conclusions about what your family played:

| What you remember | What that suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone laid a whole chain on their first turn | A chain-opening version such as RNK/Pagat; differs from selected Puremco opening |
| A double had to be covered on the same train immediately | Closer to RNK-style coverage than Puremco's extra-play-anywhere rule |
| The double's player could play elsewhere, then the next player had to cover it | Compatible with Puremco; also appears in other versions, so not a unique identifier |
| Four players each drew 15 tiles | Compatible with RNK/Pagat here; differs from the selected Puremco web table |
| One shared train, plus personal trains that opened with markers | Compatible with all three; does not distinguish an edition |

Several matches together are more informative than one. The physical box/insert, a photograph, or another family member's recollection could establish the historical version; we currently have none of that evidence. We have chosen a documented reference, **not verified that it reproduces your family's rules**.

If later memories fit another edition, record that as a deliberate baseline revision and update the rulebook, state mapping, examples, and engine plan together. Do not call a published chain-opening game “wrong Mexican Train.”

## What belongs only to this app

Simultaneous private opening construction was our earlier project proposal; it is not selected Puremco play. Sequential chain openings in a printed rulebook do not establish that simultaneous submissions are part of that edition.

Undo checkpoints, network acknowledgments, readiness buttons, automatic announcements, and the proposed treatment of tied winners are software decisions. They are labeled separately in R-015–R-017 of the [rulebook](TRAIN_DOMINOES_RULEBOOK.md). They should not be used to identify the family's table-game rules or presented as publisher requirements.

Chickenfoot is a separate domino game, with branching responses to doubles; it is not a synonym for Puremco or a shared Mexican Train. The [publisher sheet lists Chickenfoot and Mexican Train separately](https://www.universitygames.com/_files/ugd/05f72c_230047a18d1c4372a9ec2dd61b1de8f0.pdf).
